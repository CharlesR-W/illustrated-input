# Suggesting a language: the spec guide

Illustrated Input publishes graded readers in the style of Ørberg's *Lingua Latina per se
Illustrata* (LLPSI). Each book is one continuous story, told entirely in the target
language. Every new word is meant to be understood from context, a picture or a short
gloss. Exercises and an Anki deck come with each chapter.

You can ask for a course in a language you want to learn. Send a **spec**: a short
description of who the course is for, where it should lead, and what the story should be
like. The owner writes the course itself. **Please do not write
chapters, lessons or vocabulary lists**, only the spec.

This repository is generated by a build. Pull requests to it are overwritten, so a spec
arrives as a **GitHub issue** with the `language-spec` label.

---

## For the coding agent

A person has asked you to help them write a language spec for this project. Please:

1. **Interview them.** Ask about the sections below in order, 2–4 questions at a time.
   For each question, offer a sensible default and one or two concrete alternatives, so
   that "the default is fine" is a complete answer. Where taste matters (story, tone),
   offer 2–3 one-paragraph pitches rather than abstract questions. Be warm and quick:
   aim for 10–20 minutes. They may skip any section; record "no preference" and move on.
2. **Do not invent their preferences.** Mark anything you suggested that they did not
   clearly confirm as *(suggested, not confirmed)*.
3. **Show them the draft spec** in the format below and revise it until they are happy.
4. **File it.** With their permission, create the issue:
   ```
   gh issue create --repo CharlesR-W/illustrated-input --label language-spec \
     --title "Language spec: <language>" --body-file spec.md
   ```
   If `gh` is unavailable or unauthenticated, give them the text and this link to paste
   it into:
   https://github.com/CharlesR-W/illustrated-input/issues/new?template=language-spec.yml
5. Do not open pull requests, write course content or contact anyone else.

The existing courses can serve as examples; ask whether the person has looked at one.
The reader is at https://charlesr-w.github.io/illustrated-input/, and the content is
under `content/<lang>/` here (`course.json`, `lessons/`).

### Interview sections

**1. Language and learner**
- Which language, and which variety, dialect or period (e.g. Attic Greek, Brazilian
  Portuguese, Biblical Hebrew, northern or southern Welsh)?
- Their starting level, and other languages they know that might help (e.g. Latin for
  Italian, Russian for Polish). A course may build on a bridge language.
- How they would use it: on a phone, at a desk, minutes per day.

**2. Destination**
- What they want to be able to read or do by the end: a particular author or text
  (Dante, the Analects, the sagas), newspapers, conversation, travel.
- When they would like to meet real (authentic, lightly adapted) text.
- Course length, as a feel rather than a number: a short taster (≈10 chapters), a year's
  course (≈30–40), or longer.

**3. Story (the most important part)**
- Setting: place, period and social world. A setting that naturally brings in the
  destination's vocabulary works best (e.g. a medieval Florentine workshop for Dante).
- Protagonist(s): who they are, what they want and what stands in their way. Who else
  is in the cast?
- The central question that pulls the reader through the book.
- Tone: how serious, dark or funny. Anything they would find cringe-inducing.
  Known failure modes to avoid: low-stakes "community projects", mysteries about keys,
  boxes or unopened envelopes, prose that just moves objects around, adults talking like
  children, moralising.
- Offer 2–3 short pitches and let them choose or mix.

**4. Register and voice**
- Narration: past or present tense, close third person or a narrator figure.
- Politeness and register in dialogue (tu/vous, ты/вы, keigo, archaic forms).
- If you can, show two sample sentences in different voices and let them pick.

**5. Script, pronunciation and aids** (where relevant)
- Script policy (e.g. kanji with furigana from chapter 1, pinyin, transliteration,
  accent marks, vowel points).
- How much English they want: grammar notes in English, tap-to-gloss, or none.
- Pictures matter to the method; note anything they would especially like illustrated.

**6. Hard constraints and anything else**
- Historical or cultural accuracy, content limits, topics to avoid.
- Sources they love: textbooks, readers, LLPSI-like books in this language.
- How the owner can reach them with follow-up questions (optional).

### Spec format (the issue body)

```markdown
# Language spec: <language and variety>

Submitted by: <name or handle>, via <agent, if any>, <date>

## 1. Language and learner
## 2. Destination
## 3. Story
<!-- the chosen pitch, cast, central question, tone, things to avoid -->
## 4. Register and voice
## 5. Script, pronunciation and aids
## 6. Constraints, sources, other notes
## Open questions
<!-- things they were unsure about; suggestions they did not confirm -->
```

Keep it short: one to two pages is plenty. Clear preferences matter more than
completeness.
